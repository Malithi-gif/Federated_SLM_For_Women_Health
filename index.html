Research overview

Why privacy-preserving menstrual health intelligence matters

Menstrual applications collect highly sensitive information, including bleeding, pain, mood, hormone levels, sleep, fertility, and reproductive-health indicators. Centralized processing can create privacy, trust, and unauthorized-access risks.

01

Participant-level federation

Each participant is treated as an independent client, keeping raw records on the local device.

02

Two protection settings

Post-quantum-secure parameter exchange and client-level differential privacy are evaluated separately.

03

Deployment-focused evaluation

Accuracy, F1, runtime, latency, throughput, memory, energy, communication, and privacy are measured.

PQC setting

Post-quantum-secure communication

Protects global adapters and local updates during client-server transmission.

  • ML-KEM key establishment
  • HKDF-SHA256 key derivation
  • AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption
  • FedAvg after server-side decryption
DP setting

Client-level differential privacy

Limits information leakage about individual client contributions.

  • L2 clipping with C = 0.5
  • Gaussian noise multiplier σ = 0.1
  • Privacy parameter δ = 10⁻⁵
  • RDP accounting for accumulated ε

Privacy–utility interpretation

PQC and DP protect different parts of the federated pipeline

Post-quantum security

Secures updates in transit without perturbing them, preserving utility but increasing communication cost as adapter size grows.

Differential privacy

Protects individual client contributions through clipping and noise, but may reduce accuracy, add training rounds, and increase memory use.

Main finding

DistilBERT maintains 0.8432 accuracy under both settings while requiring far less training time, memory, latency, and communication than decoder models.

Important privacy caveat: the reported DP ε values are large because the experiments use a small noise multiplier to preserve model utility. These results should not be presented as strong worst-case privacy.
Limitations and future work

What remains before real-world deployment

Broader evaluation

Use larger, more diverse cohorts and additional women’s health datasets.

Stronger DP guarantees

Explore larger noise multipliers, fewer rounds, and privacy–utility tuning to reduce ε.

On-device validation

Measure performance on real mobile and wearable hardware and conduct user-centered safety studies.
